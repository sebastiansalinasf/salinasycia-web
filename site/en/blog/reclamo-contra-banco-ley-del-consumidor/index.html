When your bank fails you: how to file a claim before the Local Police Court

Sebastián Salinas Fuenzalida
Founding partner of Salinas & Cía. Abogados

A charge you never authorised. Funds the bank withholds without explanation. An answer that never comes, or that contradicts what you were told at the branch. These situations are common, and many people believe the only way out is to accept whatever the bank offers. That is not so: Chile's Consumer Protection Law (Law 19,496) also applies to banks and gives customers a concrete way to claim.

Where is the claim filed?

Individual claims for breaches of this law are filed before the Local Police Court (Juzgado de Policía Local). The law lets the consumer choose between the court of their own domicile or that of the supplier's domicile. In practice, this means you can sue the bank in your own municipality, without having to litigate where its head office is.

What can you ask for?

The same proceeding pursues two different things. First, a declaration that the bank breached the law and a fine against it. Second, an order to compensate the damage you suffered: the money lost and, where appropriate, other harm. What is available in each case depends on the facts and, above all, on the evidence.

Must you complain to SERNAC first?

No. A complaint to SERNAC, or to the bank's customer service, is voluntary. It has one advantage: while it is being processed, the limitation period is suspended. But it is not a requirement to go to court.

How long do you have?

The action to pursue the breach is time-barred two years after the breach ceased. Even so, it is best not to wait: records, emails and witnesses are lost over time.

Do you need a lawyer?

The law allows the complaint and claim before the Local Police Court to be filed without a lawyer. However, when the amount is significant, when heirs are involved or when the bank disputes that you are a “consumer”, legal advice helps present the case properly and avoid losing remedies through short deadlines.

Before filing, gather

  • Statements and receipts for the disputed transactions.
  • Emails, letters and complaint numbers with the bank.
  • The date you learned of the problem and what the bank answered.
  • Any document the bank asked you for and the date you delivered it.
